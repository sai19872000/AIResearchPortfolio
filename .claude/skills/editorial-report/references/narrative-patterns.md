# Narrative patterns: how a Pudding piece walks the reader through

These are the story mechanics behind the visuals. A report that adopts the look
but keeps a wall-of-findings structure will still read like a slide dump. The
structure is half of the theme.

## Openings (pick one)

| Pattern | Pudding example | Report translation |
|---|---|---|
| **Blunt one-line hook** | "YOU / WILL / DIE" (birthday-effect). "By almost any measure, the WNBA just had its best season ever. On Fox News, they told a very different story." | The first line of the executive summary states the single most surprising finding as a sentence, with its number |
| **Question as engine** | "What's the best way to dice an onion?", "Do more people die on their birthday?" | Open with the question the client is paying to answer, then answer it in sentence two |
| **Composite protagonist** | the median girl (womens-sizing), Henry & Mary (love-story), "Bones" (mow) | "Take a typical batch: 3 strengths, 41 tests…" Follow one representative record through the system |
| **Reader goes first** | fill-in-the-blank quiz (similes), mow the lawn (mow), pick a wine (wine-animals) | Static: "Before reading on, guess: what share of specs needed manual review? (Answer: 62%.)" Use it sparingly, at most once per document |
| **Opinionated claim** | "I believe there have only been five truly ethical NBA champions" | A recommendation stated first, then earned |
| **Personal frame** | the author's mother's TV complaint (aapi-casting), a conservative relative (wnba) | Rarely right for client work. A short "Why we looked" paragraph is the formal equivalent |

## Body mechanics

1. **Tell the reader how to read it before showing it.** "One dot is five speeches". The dek of the first figure does this.
2. **Add one variable at a time.** ethical-champions redraws the same chart once per ingredient: games missed → weighted impact → low-minute games. Never show the finished complex chart cold. In print, use a sequence of 2–4 frames of the same chart, each with one new layer, and a caption saying what changed.
3. **Decoy, then correction.** Build confidence, then "**But…**" (birthday-effect used it 4 times). This is how you present a naive metric and then the adjusted one. It is honest and it holds attention.
4. **Zoom: overview → subset → individual.** 1,700 conversations → one pair → one quote. All 80 films → one film → one actor.
5. **Caveat inline, detail at the end.**
   - Define a term where it first matters, in one or two sentences ("How did we define a motif?").
   - Long method goes in the appendix.
6. **Show the reader's own case against the crowd.** Your guess against the data, your path against 30,954 others. In a report this is "your plant against the benchmark set".
7. **Calibrate numbers in words.** "+6 is MVP tier". A number with no anchor is noise.
8. **The section heading is a finding, not a topic.**
   - Write "Fox covers Clark 3.5× more than her peers", not "Coverage analysis".
   - In a client deliverable keep the numbered section label, then the finding: `## 3. Specs split cleanly by product family`.

## Endings

- **Return to the hook.** Answer the opening question explicitly, with the final number.
- **"So what" paragraph.** "This coverage matters" (wnba). In a report this is the recommendation and next step.
- **Explore mode.** Interactive pieces end in free exploration. A static report ends in an appendix table with everything.
- **Methods, data and thanks**, in that order:
  - Methods covers sources, sample sizes, filters and cut-offs, models and prompts used.
  - Any **AI-generated content is labelled as such**.
  - Limitations get their own short list.

## Voice

- Plain, concrete, conversational, but exact. Short sentences carry numbers.
- Humour and film references appear in culture pieces, but **not** in client deliverables. Keep the clarity and drop the jokes.
- First person in Pudding is a feature. In client work use "we" (the team) sparingly and never as filler.
- Every sentence earns its place. If a paragraph does not set up, explain or qualify a figure, cut it. (See the operator rule: no AI slop.)

## Pacing guide for a 10–20 page report

| Section | Share | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Hook + summary | 1 page | The finding, 3 stat numbers, the recommendation |
| How to read / scope | ½ page | Data, period, units, "one row = one…" |
| Findings | 60% | 3–6 sections, each a claim-heading, one hero figure, 1–3 paragraphs |
| Recommendation | 1 page | What to do, owner, effort, risk |
| Methods + appendix | rest | Full tables, definitions, limitations, provenance |
